# Quality bar (check before delivering)

- **Claims ledger** (in section 20). List the 10–15 claims the conclusion rests on. For each, give:
    - the claim
    - its type: fact (disclosed), signal (seen in public data), or inference
    - its sources
    - whether it is triangulated (y/n)
    - confidence (high, medium or low)

  Any low-confidence claim that the conclusion depends on must appear in "What you'd have to believe".
- **Citations.** Every fact links to a page you actually opened. Never cite a search snippet. List the sources at the end.
- **Labels.** Mark every inference. Mark vendor-run benchmarks as unverified. Be suspicious of relative dates on pages ("6 days ago"), which may be stale.
- **Freshness.** Date-stamp time-sensitive facts. Prefer sources from the last 12 months, and flag anything older.
- **Balance.** Report how often a theme comes up and how varied its sources are. Never let one viral thread carry a theme. Say that public forums skew negative.
- **Methodology.** Cover:
    - the sources searched, with counts
    - the time window
    - blocked or missing data
    - confidence per section
    - the primary research that would raise confidence the most
- **Arithmetic.** Every score, scenario and revenue number comes from `scripts/score.py`, never mental math.
- **Formats.**
    - Use tables for comparisons.
    - Use Mermaid for the architecture.
    - Use charts for moat scores, opportunity priority and scenarios, when the output medium supports charts.
    - Put formulas in code blocks.
- **Style.** The first sentence of each section states its conclusion. Keep sentences short and use specific figures rather than adjectives.
- **Footer.** "Public sources only. Estimates are labeled. Not investment advice."
